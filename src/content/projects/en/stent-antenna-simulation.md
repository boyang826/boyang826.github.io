## The question

Endovascular devices such as stent electrodes can reach the brain without open surgery. If the stent itself could receive power wirelessly from outside the head, it could also act as a stimulation electrode with no battery and no wires through the skull.

## What I did

- **Reviewed the field.** I read recent work on wireless-powered endovascular stents and compared two powering approaches: near-field inductive coupling at around 100 kHz, and radiative coupling at around 1–1.5 GHz. I presented the comparison to the lab.
- **Built a simulation.** I set up a CST simulation of wireless RF power delivery to a fourth-generation intracranial vascular stent, using a spiral antenna design and 3D human head models with brain and skull layers.
- **Looked at the skull.** I used the simulation to study how the skull attenuates the RF energy that reaches the stent.

## Status

This was an early, simulation-only study. For me it was a fast introduction to RF tools and the physics of wireless power, not a finished design.
